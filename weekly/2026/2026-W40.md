## 1. What survived

**Megacap tech kept leading while equity volatility stayed calm, whatever drove the day (H5 + H4).** QQQ beat SPY in every session from Sep 29 to Oct 2, and VIX never closed above 16.39. Each day threw a different test at it:

- **Sep 30, rates.** The 10Y went to 5.29%, matching its 2002 high (Treasury), and stocks sold off in the final hour. QQQ still closed +0.25% while SPY fell −0.21% and RSP −0.71%. VIX was 16.34.
- **Oct 1, breadth.** This was the hardest test. The 10Y touched 5.34% intraday, ISM prices paid jumped to 77.9 (72.3 consensus) and oil rose. IWM (+0.41%) and RSP (+0.47%) beat QQQ, and GOOG and AAPL fell. QQQ still beat SPY (+0.31% vs +0.18%), and VIX was 16.39.
- **Oct 2, macro shock.** Payrolls came in at +29k against a +90k consensus. Yields went from down to up by the close (2Y +5 bp, 10Y +4 bp). QQQ rose +1.02% against SPY's +0.74%, and VIX fell to 15.31.

Two limits on that claim:
- The leaders were chips and software, not every megacap; GOOG and AAPL fell on Oct 1.
- Three journal sessions is a small sample.

**What broke: the gold bid as a premarket signal.** The brief led with gold twice:
- Sep 30: "Gold and miners lead a firmer premarket". GLD closed −0.54% and GDX −1.43%.
- Oct 2: "Megacap tech and gold both bid strongly". GLD went from +1.33% premarket to −0.68% at the close.

Both times the bid failed after the open, and GLD lost −3.37% on the week. A second link was strained on Oct 2: weak jobs data should have lowered yields, but the front end closed higher and stocks rose anyway.
